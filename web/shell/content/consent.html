What is this study about?

We are studying how people find their way around websites, and how the body responds while they do so. The results will help design websites that adapt to the people using them.

What will I do?

The session takes about 50 minutes. You will watch short videos or do short activities, use a website to find information, and answer brief questions about how you feel. The website's menu and layout will vary between parts. Some parts are designed to influence how you feel, for example by being challenging, repetitive or emotional. Everything will be fully explained at the end.

What is recorded?

  • Where you look on the screen, and your pupil size (eye tracker)
  • Skin conductance from two small sensors on your fingers, and your heart rate
  • Your clicks, mouse movements, answers and ratings on screen, and which menu you were using

No video or audio of you is recorded.

Are there any risks?

Some activities may feel mildly stressful, frustrating or boring, and some video clips may make you feel sad or amused. These feelings are brief. You can stop at any time; just tell the researcher.

What happens to my data?

Your data is stored under a participant code, never your name, on a secure university computer. It will be used for research and to train computer models, and may appear in anonymised, group-level form in publications. You can ask for your data to be deleted up to the end of the session, or later by quoting your participant code.
